AI first, on your terms

paperus has no chatbot of its own and calls no AI company. It does what a library should: find, check, keep track. The thinking happens in the assistant you choose, and it can only ever reach what you can.

paperus, on your server

  • Reads every PDF, page by page, and remembers where each passage is
  • Searches by meaning, with a small open model that runs right there
  • Decides who may see what, and logs every change an assistant makes

Your assistant, your choice

  • Reasons, compares, summarises and helps you write
  • Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor, or a model you run yourself
  • Asks you before it changes anything in the library

Made for research groups

Your server, your group

One library for the lab, groups inside groups, and private space for each member. Everyone logs in with your organisation's OIDC login. No storage limits, no subscription, and the papers never leave your hands.

Search in your own words

Describe what you remember and find the passage, across the full text of every paper, note and annotation, by meaning as well as by keyword.

PDFs that fit your phone

A paper's two columns become one, running down the screen without margins. Figures stay whole, and highlights and notes work as on the page. No pinching, no scrolling sideways.

Preprints that get published

When a preprint in your library appears in a journal, paperus finds the published version by itself, tells you, and cites that one from then on.

Get paperus

On your server

One Linux machine with Docker and about 4 GB of memory. Put an HTTPS proxy in front, and the first person to open it sets it up. 

# compose.yaml and its settings
curl -LO https://raw.githubusercontent.com/qcode-uni-a/paperus/main/deploy/compose.yaml
curl -Lo .env https://raw.githubusercontent.com/qcode-uni-a/paperus/main/deploy/example.env
# set the folders and secrets in .env, then
docker compose up -d
The installation guide

On your computer

The app opens your libraries in a window of their own, adds PDFs you open with it, and tells you when someone shares a paper.

Download the app

    Not signed yet: Windows asks once whether to run it (More info, then Run anyway), and on a Mac you open it the first time with a right click, then Open.

    In your browser

    Save the paper you are reading, with its PDF, to your library or to a group's.

    Add to Firefox

    For Chrome and Edge, until it is in their store: load it from the release.
